# Inbound

Inbound = operators find Roveboard and come to you. For a niche B2B tool, inbound is a set of small, compounding sources, not one big channel: search and AI (see `seo.md` and `geo.md`), the founder's LinkedIn, a useful template, review sites, communities, partners and referrals. All of them lead to one place: **a 20-day free trial with a fast first win**.

## 1. Founder-led LinkedIn (main inbound channel)

Travel-trade decision makers are on LinkedIn. Posts from a real person get far more reach and trust than a company page.
- **Profile:** a headline about the customer ("Helping tour operators track every supplier, deposit and deadline | Founder, Roveboard"), a banner with the one-liner and URL, a Featured section (the template, a 2-minute demo video, the pricing page), and the company page linked as current role.
- **Cadence:** 2–3 posts a week, plus 15–20 minutes a day commenting thoughtfully on posts by operators, DMCs, travel associations and travel-tech people. Comments build visibility faster than posts at the start.
- **Post types that work in B2B:** a lesson from an operator conversation (anonymised); "the 5 supplier deadlines that…" checklists (a carousel/PDF document post); a build-in-public update with a screenshot of a real, *built* feature; a short opinion with a clear stance ("Your spreadsheet isn't the problem. Not knowing what's due this week is"); a customer story (with permission); a behind-the-scenes founder post (registering the company, the first paying customer).
- **Rules:** a hook in the first 2 lines; one idea per post; no external link in the post body if testing shows it cuts reach (put it in the first comment, and test both); end with a question that invites operators to comment; never engagement-bait.
- **Company page:** reshare the founder's posts, post product updates and the blog. Invite your network to follow it once (LinkedIn gives pages a limited number of monthly invites).
- **Repurposing:** each blog post becomes 3–5 LinkedIn posts (key insight, checklist carousel, a quote, the template).
- **Measure:** profile views, follower growth from ICP companies, comments from operators, DMs, and sign-ups with `utm_source=linkedin`.

## 2. Lead magnets

- **Primary:** the group trip supplier and deposit tracker template (see `blog.md`). Ungated download plus an optional email opt-in.
- **Later:** a "group trip planning timeline" checklist (PDF), a "supplier contract terms cheat sheet" (release dates, attrition, deposits, cancellation), a costing calculator. Each one must be useful without Roveboard and connect naturally to it.

## 3. Newsletter ("product updates" list)

The `waitlist` table already collects "Get product updates" subscribers with consent. Once there's a domain and an email tool:
- Send **monthly**: one useful idea (from the blog or interviews), what's new in Roveboard (built features only), and one question to reply to. Plain text, from the founder's name. Replies are gold; answer every one.
- Every email: a clear sender, the reason they're receiving it, and a one-click unsubscribe (the law, and the inbox providers' rules).
- Only email people who opted in. Cold contacts go through outbound rules, not the newsletter.

## 4. Review sites and directories

- **Profiles:** G2, Capterra (one listing covers GetApp and Software Advice), Product Hunt, Crunchbase, SaaSHub, AlternativeTo, BetaList, plus travel-tech directories and association supplier lists if eligible. Use the identical one-liner and boilerplate from `positioning.md`.
- **Reviews:** after a customer's first successful trip (or 30 days of active use), the founder personally asks for an honest review with a direct link. Never offer payment for positive reviews; platform-sanctioned incentives (e.g. gift cards offered for any review, positive or negative) are allowed only per each site's rules. Aim for 10 reviews in the first 6 months.
- **Paid placements** (Capterra pay-per-click, G2 paid tiers): not until the trial converts well and you know what a customer is worth.

## 5. Communities and events

- **Associations:** AITO, ABTA, ETOA (UK/Europe); ASTA and USTOA (US); regional DMC networks. Many have supplier or partner memberships, events and newsletters. Evaluate cost against access to members; often a member's introduction is worth more than the membership.
- **Online groups:** LinkedIn and Facebook groups for tour operators, DMCs and groups travel; Reddit. Be useful first (answer questions fully); mention Roveboard only when it truly answers the question, and say you're the founder.
- **Events:** WTM London (usually November), ITB Berlin (usually March), plus smaller regional trade shows and association conferences. As a visitor, not an exhibitor, at first: book meetings in advance via LinkedIn. Check dates and visitor pass costs each year.
- **Webinars/workshops (later):** "Run your group-trip suppliers like a pro" with a partner association or a known operator; record it and turn it into content.

## 6. Partnerships

- **Complementary tools** (per "integrate, don't build"): itinerary builders (Travefy, Tourwriter), accounting (Xero, QuickBooks), booking platforms. Start with a co-written guide or a listing in their app marketplace once an integration exists.
- **Consultants, accountants and trainers who serve small tour operators:** a referral arrangement (e.g. a revenue share or free months for their clients). Write it down simply, and make sure it's compliant and disclosed.
- **Associations:** a member discount in exchange for newsletter features and event slots.

## 7. Referrals

Once customers are happy: "Give a month, get a month" (both the referrer and the new company get a free month). Ask at moments of delight (first trip fully confirmed, first export, a renewal). Build it only after billing is live and there are 10+ active customers; until then, ask personally.

## 8. Website conversion and the trial journey

Traffic is wasted if the trial doesn't produce a first win. **Activation = a company that has created a trip and added 3+ suppliers with at least one deadline** (refine with data).
- **On the site:** one primary CTA ("Start your 20-day free trial"), a secondary "Book a 15-minute walkthrough", proof near CTAs (pilot quotes, screenshots), and the price visible.
- **Trial emails** (from the founder's name, plain text; build once Resend or Loops and the domain are set up; propose the sequence first):

| Day | Email | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Welcome: "Here's the fastest way to set up your first trip (import your spreadsheet)" | First trip |
| 1 | If no trip yet: "Want me to import your spreadsheet for you?" (concierge offer) | Activation |
| 3 | "See everything you owe at a glance": money summary and deadlines | Value |
| 7 | A short story: how an operator avoids missed deposits (real, with permission) | Habit |
| 14 | "How's it going?" Personal check-in; ask for a 15-minute call | Feedback, sales |
| 17 | Trial ends in 3 days: what you keep, the plans, the yearly discount | Conversion |
| 20 | Trial ended: choose a plan; your data is safe and exportable | Conversion |

Make each email conditional on what the user has done (skip the setup email if they've already set up). Track open → click → activation → paid for each step.

- **Concierge onboarding:** offer to import the customer's spreadsheet for them. It's the strongest activation tactic for a small team, and it teaches you about their data.

## Inbound metrics (weekly)

Visitors (by source), template downloads, newsletter sign-ups, trial sign-ups, activation rate, trial → paid, walkthrough calls booked, and "How did you hear about us?" answers.
